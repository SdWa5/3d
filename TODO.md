## The goal

As many *sensible* speaker configurations as possible, generated automatically by a single command in its default
settings, with priority on the configurations actually used in praxis as well as ones algorithmically derived.

Not only enforce the subwoofer ceiling strictly (optimum 2–3 m) but improve the existing logic to reach it: **do not
avoid generating a scene, ignore the ceiling, or use fewer speakers if there is any other possibility to solve it.**

**Two rulings by the owner decide how to read that.** The sub height band is an aim rather than a gate, so a wall that
puts the tops below or above head height is a miss written on the file and never a refusal (CVR-7). And how wide a scene
comes out limits nothing unless a width is explicitly passed (CVR-8). Both are built.

The committed inventories hold **2219 scenes** as of 0.124.0. Every stack's sub/top transition is aimed at a target
height, 2.5 m unless the command or an event states another, and the miss is written on the file. Every refusal is named
and every shape rule is stated in metres rather than in cabinet counts. What is still refused is geometry and
duplicates, grouped below by cause.

**Two findings from the CHANGELOG govern what to build next.** The orientation axis is worth more than every other axis
put together, because a rolled sub wall is short enough for the band where an upright one is not. And the borrowed-gear
pairs beat every single owner.

## How to read this

* **One table per group.** Groups are by shared root cause, so finishing one closes several rows.
* **Everything is sorted by priority.** Rows inside a table are sorted by Prio, and a section sits where its own top row
  does. Ties keep the order they already had rather than being re-argued.
* **IDs are stable and never renumbered**, so `GEO-2` keeps meaning `GEO-2` when rows are added, moved or deleted.
* **Prio.** `P1` blocks the goal above. `P2` is a real defect or a wanted feature. `P3` is done when the area is next
  touched. `P4` is wanted, but nothing waits on it and it may sit for a long time. `P5` and beyond is a direction rather
  than a task, kept because knowing where the project might go changes how the things above it are built.
* **Effort** is one focused pass including tests and docs, to 5 minutes. `phys` means physical work (tape measure,
  hanging scale, opening a rack), which no estimate can shorten.
* **Buys** is the measured payoff. `—` means it buys nothing measurable and is wanted for its own sake.
* **Needs** names the IDs that must land first, or `decision` when a question has to be answered before any code.
* **State** is `open`, `partial` (some of it shipped), `measured` (tried, with the result recorded), `decision`
  (blocked on a call rather than on work), `known` (a fact worth keeping, not a task) or `proposal` (suggested by Claude
  and not yet agreed by the owner, so nothing is built until it is).
* **Measured figures are re-measured when touched**, never carried forward on trust. Several were wrong for two
  releases.
* **This file is never split, however long it gets**, and neither are `README.md` and `CHANGELOG.md`. Stated by the
  owner. When it gets unwieldy it is compacted first, and only genuine reference material moves into `docs/`.
* **Runtime is not a constraint on this project**, stated by the user. No estimate, design or priority here may trade
  coverage for speed. Measure it, record it, and do not treat it as a blocker.
* **GEO, SYM and ALN together are the highest priority**, because geometry, symmetry and alignment are one placement
  problem seen from three sides. While working inside any of them, fix every bug hit and implement every related item it
  turns up immediately rather than filing it for later.
* **Every silhouette rule is a width in metres, never a cabinet count**, stated by the owner for the pyramid, the V and
  the tower alike. They all live in `StackChecks::silhouetteProblem()`, and anything new goes there too.

#### GEO-11 — the three stages that cannot see each other

Where: `StackSolver::fillWith()` and `packedRows()`, `Gravity::resolve()`, `SceneCompiler`.

**The fill decides row widths, `Gravity` decides where cabinets land, the compiler decides final x, and no stage sees
the next one's answer.** It was re-derived four times from four symptoms, each time by somebody who did not know it was
the same thing:

| found as  | what it looked like                                                                                                                     |
|-----------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **GEO-2** | a fill was moved sideways after gravity had seated it and kept the old height, leaving a top 228 mm in the air                          |
| **GEO-4** | a row slides for its own bearing and walks out from under the tier it carries, which no bound in stack clearance can see                |
| **GEO-5** | a width-based pyramid narrows rows, narrow rows make more of them, and gravity splits that staircase into runs 127 mm inside each other |

Each was attacked on its own and each was measured net negative or inert. That is the evidence that they are one
problem.

**0.136.1 measured it from the tops' side.** Shared tops are dealt after the sub solve, so the per-stack ranking
cannot see where they land. Once `low` paid for height and the pyramid gained a second fill order, 32 regenerated rigs
missed their target by more without carrying another cabinet, 16.5 m in total, and 27 of them share their tops, for
14.7 m. The largest is `innschleife-psl-sdwa5-sepp/stacked-1-tops-shared---v-------mixed---alternate-center-low`
and its `column` twin, where the Innschleife stack falls into one flat row and the tops sit 1400 mm lower than in
0.136.0. Five `central` rigs with shared tops became impossible the same way.

**The stack-local half shipped in 0.83.0 as an injected predicate.** `StackSolver` stays free of the compiler, and
`SceneCompiler::seatingCheck()` answers from the same `orientationFor()` and `worldBox()` the finished scene uses. The
CHANGELOG has the defects it cost and the measurement. **One invariant broke three times in new places and now has a
test of its own.** The solve and the compile must be given the same stack and asked the same question, which includes
`slide_slack_m` and the tops' `aim`.

**The scene-level half is the one item in the file worth a plan before a line of code.** Two shapes are worth weighing.
A **two-pass compile** solves the whole rig, runs gravity and the compiler, and re-runs the fill knowing the answer. A
**lookahead inside the fill** speculatively seats each candidate row before accepting it. The first is simpler and
slower, and the second is faster and duplicates knowledge. Design it knowing VIS-1, because one compile per accepted
arrangement is affordable in a batch and not inside a frame.

#### GEO-14 — low and central

Where: `StackSolver::fill()` and `spreadRows()`, `SceneStackCommand::heightCost()`, `StackMetrics::centred()`,
`StackTops::topRow()`, `StackDeal::byType()`.

**Settled by the owner:**

* **The shapes keep priority, and the acoustic rule is an optimisation rather than a gate.** Nothing is refused for it
  and no rig gets narrower.
* **The key is frequency, not weight**, and frequency decides only between two cabinets that both state a passband. Nine
  of our ten speakers state none, so a rule that ranked on absence would rank almost everything on nothing.
* **"Central" is the rig's centre line for `center` and `block`, and each stack's own for `stereo`.** That mirrors the
  split `topRow()` already makes for tops.
* **Height and acoustics are traded off as differently weighted metrics**, so a rig may give up some height to put the
  low end lower and more central, or the reverse.

**Central inside a row can only ever be a weighted preference.** `centred()` puts the tallest take in the middle,
`widestSub()` the widest sub and `topRow()` the long throw, and each of them is load-bearing for a reason that is not
acoustics. The tall segment in the middle carries the row above, measured at 14 % bearing when it sits outboard instead.
So a frequency rule ranks what the bearing rules accept and never replaces their key.

**Central across stacks has no incumbent rule**, which is why it is the quarter to build. `byType()` balances the split
on `quantity × width` alone, and `$index === $middle` in `inventoryFor()` is the inner/outer vocabulary to build it on.

**The objective becomes one weighted score.** `heightCost()` already holds the distance from `target_sub_height_m` and
`OUT_OF_BAND_PENALTY` times the part of the miss outside the band. The acoustic measures go beside them, each scored so
that lower is better and each weighted separately. They are how far the lowest cabinets sit above the floor, weighted by
how low and how powerful they are, and how far those cabinets sit from the centre line.

**The one open sub-question is where the weights live**, and it decides whether a scene can be reproduced from its own
file. A constant is the smallest thing that works and allows no trade-off per rig. A CLI option is reproducible through
the recorded command and adds an axis the sweep would want to walk. A scene key is the most expressive and puts a search
parameter into the schema, which the `max_width_m` work argued against.

#### GEO-4 — the row that may not move

Where: `Gravity::slidSeats()` (bounded by `Stack::$slideSlackM`), `Stack::spreadApart()`, `SceneCompiler`.

A row does not have to be centred on what carries it, and 0.70.0 already slides one where nothing stands beside the
stack. Between stacks the bound `max(0.0, clearance / 2 - gap)` follows from `StackSceneWriter::centres` and removes
every interpenetration the unbounded slide caused. It still loses net:

|               | scenes | `LEFT OUT` | nothing-under-at-all |
|---------------|--------|------------|----------------------|
| off (shipped) | **11** | 2          | 3                    |
| on, bounded   | 10     | **0**      | 6                    |

It gains `stacked-gmss-2-center` and clears both left-out cabinets, and it loses `stacked-all-2-center` and
`stacked-all-2-stereo`.

**A one-tier lookahead in `slidSeats()` did not move the trade either.** It models the tier above at its nominal seats,
while the real tops row is moved later by `throwFirst()`, the `clear_of` fills and `spreadApart()`, all in the compiler.
It is kept because it is correct and inert. On the way it fixed `worstBearing()` reading a floating cabinet as perfectly
carried, which `Gravity::carriedBearing()` now avoids and `GravityTest` pins. **The blocker is GEO-11**, because gravity
decides support before the compiler decides final x.

#### GEO-5 — one negative result worth not repeating

A *pure* width bound with no shoulder allowance was tried twice and reverted twice. Bounding by the row below gave 7
scenes against 11 with 2 interpenetrations, and bounding by the base gave 8 with 3. What works is
`PYRAMID_SHOULDER = 0.1` of the outboard cabinet per side. Why the pure bound fails is GEO-11. Narrower rows make more
rows, and `Gravity` splits a wall of many thin rows into runs inside each other.

**What is left, and none of it is a keyboard job:**

1. **The Movano on a scale.** It is half the fleet payload, and an error in it points at a fine rather than a wasted
   trip. One weighbridge ticket settles it.
2. **A tape measure inside both bays.** Length at the floor, width between the walls and between the arches, height
   under the roof and through the rear door aperture, and anything bolted in.
3. **One look at Sepp's roof**, which settles L3H2 against L3H3 and 236 mm of bay height. His papers state no dimensions

**The trap that cost 365 kg.** A registration document is authoritative about what a vehicle **may** weigh and merely
historical about what it **does**. Field F.2 is the law and no scale can supply it. Field G dates from type approval,
and a fit-out added afterwards appears in no field of the document, so **a payload needs both sources**. Austrian
`Eigengewicht` excludes the driver where German field `G` includes 75 kg, so a mass is normalised before a spec derives
payload as `F.2 − G`. The Ducato has two seats and the Movano three, and both stored masses assume a driver and nobody
else.

#### LOAD-5 — the trailer, and why 750 kg is not enough

Stated by the owner on 2026-08-17, before the purchase. **It is a generator trailer, not spare space**, because it
brings 550 kg of capacity and 465 kg of new load with it. Closing the remaining 134.9 kg needs a trailer payload near
680 kg, so roughly 900 to 1000 kg gross and braked. Both vans tow that, with O1 at 3000 kg braked on the Ducato and 2500
on the Movano, but a combination above 3500 + 750 kg has licence implications worth checking before buying. 750 kg is
exactly O2, the unbraked limit on both sets of papers, so today the trailer is legal by construction rather than by
check.

It is specced with `quantity: 1` on the owner's instruction before it arrived, and its notes say so. Correct it the day
it either arrives or does not.

**What the schema is missing:**

* **Towing capacity.** O1 and O2 are on both sets of papers and in no field, so nothing checks a trailer's laden mass
  against what tows it.
* **Which vehicle tows which trailer**, which is a pairing rather than a property of either.
* **An open bed has no height**, so the trailer states no `load_bay_m`, because a side height would be read as a
  ceiling. Nothing distinguishes "open" from "nobody measured it".

#### TOOL-19 — the regeneration diff, and the four ways to cut it

The scenes are committed so that any one of them can be opened on GitHub and read, so every option keeps the files in a
repository somebody can browse. `.git` is not the problem, because YAML this repetitive packs to almost nothing, so any
argument for moving the tree rests on reviewability rather than size.

* **One commit per kind, as a rule.** A commit is either code or regeneration and never both. It costs nothing and fixes
  the one thing that actually hurts.
* **`diff.renameLimit`.** One config line makes `git log --stat` read a folder rename as renames again. It does not
  reduce the diff, only the lie about it.
* **A submodule.** `scenes/generated/` becomes its own repository and the parent records one pointer per regeneration.
  That is the only option that pairs a code commit exactly with the scenes it produced, and it makes a fresh clone a
  two-step affair.
* **A branch of its own.** The tree lives on `scenes` and never on `main`, so `main`'s history is pure code, and the
  pairing becomes a convention in a commit message rather than something git enforces.

The first two are compatible with each other and with either structural option, which is what the 3h assumes.

#### SIG-1 — the routing that lived in a spreadsheet

The hardware it describes is **two DSPs**, an 8×8 and a Behringer DCX2496, and **four amplifiers**, a
Tulun/Play/Prokustk TIP10000q, a GISEN MM14K, a GISEN M60D and a Behringer EP4000.

**The repository becomes master and the workbook becomes generated output**, stated by the owner after asking whether a
two-way sync was possible. It is the wrong tool regardless. A spreadsheet has no merge, the remote is read-only (SIG-4),
`Vspk_RMS_V` is a formula a round trip would drop, and the Drive already shows the drift: four files all named
`AmpLimiterCalc.csv` sit in one folder with three distinct sizes, re-measured on 2026-09-08 at 7700, 7718 and 7432
bytes. Reconciling them is a reading job rather than a delete.

**What it touches that the repository already has opinions about:**

* **SPEC-13's figures are sourced** for all six groups. `audio.power_w` exists since 0.137.0, so the sheet's RMS
  wattage imports into it, and impedance still needs a field.
* **SPEC-8 is half answered**, see its row.
* **Provenance applies per figure.** The sheet is a working sheet rather than a datasheet. It disagrees with
  `docs/sources.md` in four places, recorded there, and with a CSV beside it about amplifier gain, so an import must not
  quietly pick a side.
* **`nuke`'s "8 turbo subs 3000rms" is one rating covering two cabinet types**, already recorded as unsplittable. A
  routing model has to be able to say that rather than force a number per box.
* **Cabling is geometry as well as topology.** The sheet holds no cable lengths, so whether SIG-1 models runs from
  placement or records stated lengths is still a decision.

## VIS · the long view

| ID    | Item                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Prio | Effort | Buys | Needs | State |

| VIS-1 | **Make the whole thing an interactive real-time simulator.** Move the rig in a viewport and see the solve, the coverage and the stability answer as you go, instead of editing a scene file and waiting on Blender. A direction rather than a task | P5   | —      | —    | —     | open  |

**Recorded because it changes how the things above it are built.**

* **Runtime stops being free once this is on the table.** A batch sweep may be slow, and a simulator may not, so
  TOOL-9's ladder pruning and the TOOL-10 to TOOL-14 caching rows are its groundwork. Parallelism is not, because 28
  cores fix a batch and do nothing for a single solve that has to land inside a frame.
* **Re-solving from constraints is the right shape for it.** A scene records what the rig has to satisfy rather than
  where the cabinets ended up, so a viewport that moves a stack asks the same solver the same question.
* **The seating check's cost is the warning.** One compile per accepted arrangement does not fit at 60 Hz, so GEO-11's
  scene-level half should be designed knowing that.
